Drop rule · all sources

Warn when a check returns less than half of its lowest count in the last 14 checks, or 0 when it normally has 3 or more.

A warning never closes jobs. They stay open until you confirm the drop is real, or until the next 2 checks agree.

A failed check (timeout, error page, login wall) changes nothing at all.

Design notes
  • One page with a section rail (a row of tabs at 400px), not six sub-screens: Kris arrives from a banner to fix one thing, and the rail plus anchors take him straight there. Rail items get a red or amber dot when their section needs attention.
  • The sources table is source health: every row shows last check, count, the source's normal range and a status chip with text, never colour alone. A quiet source with a normal of 0 (Amazon in Bulgaria) shows "Quiet, as usual", so zero isn't confused with broken.
  • "Jobs found" splits in-location from total, so a big global board like Canonical doesn't look alarming or broken.
  • The drop rule is written out in plain words next to the history, because it's what decides when the app raises its voice.
  • Mailbox shows the forwarding chain as a path, which makes "where did it break?" readable in the error state. Model & budget shows which tasks send personal data, since that's the privacy question behind host pinning.
  • Delete is the only destructive control and sits apart in a red-bordered box, with export offered first.
Design notes
  • Error format: what happened (no reply, 3 times), why (their site, not ours), what to do (Try now, open the site, pause).
  • The right-hand box answers the real question, "what does this do to my Feed?", with numbers: kept, still covered elsewhere, at risk.
  • Retry is inline on the card with a cancel; the raw request log is behind a disclosure for debugging, not in the main text.
  • Backoff (hourly after 3 failures) and the 3-day "remove it?" prompt are stated so the behaviour is predictable. Nothing is removed automatically.
Design notes
  • Different from a failure: the source answered, but with a suspicious number. The copy says that explicitly, because the fix differs (re-detect the ATS rather than retry).
  • "It's real: close them" is a ghost button so it's never the obvious click; the reason that will be stored is shown before you press it.
  • A default path exists if Kris ignores it (close after 3 zero checks in a row, with a Feed line), so the warning can't go stale forever.
Design notes
  • One field. Name or URL both work; the app works out the ATS. Fewer fields, and Kris never needs to know what a board slug is.
  • Suggestions from the researched Sofia watchlist sit below, with ATS and local volume, so adding a known company is one click.
Design notes
  • About 10 seconds, so: determinate progress, a time estimate, and each system's result appearing live in a polite live region.
  • A hit is shown as soon as it happens, with a "stop and use it" shortcut; detection continues by default because companies sometimes split across two systems.
  • The result says how many jobs land in the Feed versus kept-but-hidden, before anything is added.
Design notes
  • Not a dead end: three ranked alternatives, the email alert first because it's the compliant route for sites apply-kit won't scrape.
  • The company is still saved as "watched via alerts", so coverage checks can notice if its jobs never arrive.
Design notes
  • Fixes the old prototype's orphaned postings: removal closes the source's jobs with the reason "source removed", and the dialog says exactly how many, before you confirm.
  • Jobs also found on another source stay open; packages and Applications are never touched.
  • The primary button names its consequence ("Remove and close 7 jobs"). Undo for 10 seconds afterwards; "Pause" is offered as the softer option.
Design notes
  • Turns the riskiest assumption (coverage) into a weekly 2-minute ritual. Kris searches as usual and only reports what he searched and how many results he saw.
  • Each missing job explains why it was missed, and the fix targets the cause (watch a category), not only the symptom (add the job).
  • For sites apply-kit can't fetch (LinkedIn), the count is still useful, and the extension can capture the results page.
Design notes
  • Three-step setup with a stepper; each step is completable on its own and Back keeps what was typed.
  • The test checks the whole path (iCloud rule → mailbox → apply-kit) with a code in the email, because a login test alone wouldn't catch a broken forwarding rule.
  • Read-only and "never touches iCloud" are said at the point of entering a password, where the worry happens.
Design notes
  • The chain shows which link broke (forwarding is fine, reading isn't), so Kris knows he only needs a new app password.
  • The error sits under the field it concerns, linked with aria-describedby. "No email is lost" leads the side box because that's the first fear.
Design notes
  • Warns at 80% with a projected date, and explains the spend: a one-off spike reads very differently from a pace problem.
  • Raising the cap is scoped to this month by default, so a one-off decision doesn't quietly become the new normal.
Design notes
  • Splits into "still working" and "paused", because the fear is that the whole app stops. Discovery and filling don't need the model, so they carry on.
  • Unscreened jobs are shown, never hidden: hiding needs a dealbreaker, and with no screening there is none. Queued work runs first when the cap lifts.
  • The cap is enforced twice (in apply-kit and as the OpenRouter key's limit), stated so Kris can trust "hard".
