Design notes
  • Focal point is the "Next step" box (the only element with an ink border). Every state answers "what now?" before anything else.
  • The stepper shows the happy path Applied → Screening → Interviewing → Offer. Rejected and Withdrawn sit after an "or" as a branch, never as steps. The select beside it is the manual override.
  • Every timeline Event names its cause, "by email" or "by you", plus "by apply-kit" for schedule and source events. That's how Kris can trust a Status he didn't set.
  • The side column holds the Package exactly as sent (read-only, "View as sent"), the target posting with a saved copy, and notes.
  • Follow-up due dates are starting defaults (7/3/2/1 days after Applied/Screening/Interviewing/Offer), shown as such wherever a date appears.
Design notes
  • Polish-only: the app drafts and Kris sends. Primary is "Copy", then "Open in Mail" (recipient and thread pre-filled). "Mark sent" logs the Event. Nothing here can send.
  • Draft rules from the old prototype: body only, 70–110 words, one real detail from the posting, no fawning phrases, numbers only from the Master profile. The help line says where the number came from.
  • Snooze is 3 days (old default). Edits to the draft are learned as voice.
  • The closed posting appears as a neutral fact with reassurance, so it doesn't read as a rejection.
Design notes
  • The AI budget cap is a hard stop (PRODUCT.md). The error says what happened, when it resets, and that only drafting is affected.
  • Recovery: a deterministic outline built from the Application's own data, with no model call, so Kris can still send a good nudge in two minutes. Typed text is saved as he goes.
Design notes
  • Scheduling first: when, format, who, which round, time zone. All parsed from the invite email; Kris checks it against the email itself.
  • The prep sheet is deliberately light (PRODUCT.md: no full coaching). Company facts carry source tags and "[unverified]". Each likely question maps to a posting Requirement and to a real story from the Master profile.
  • When no truthful story exists, the row says so and gives no invented answer (honesty line). Answers written here flow back into the Master profile.
  • The old cadence sends the thank-you 2 days after Interviewing begins. Here it anchors on the interview itself. See open questions.
Design notes
  • Low-drama by design: one quoted line from the email, a one-line lesson, an optional reason pre-filled from the fit check, and Skip is always available.
  • The reason is labelled a guess for form letters, so Stats don't over-read them. "What this changes in Stats" frames the rejection as data: the response rate doesn't drop, because a rejection is a reply.
  • Recurring reasons across rejections ("2 of 3: missing skill") are the learning loop the old prototype had. Here they stay small and factual.
Design notes
  • The Offer Follow-up is a note to self, not an email: deadline, one question and one action, per the old prototype's rule.
  • Offer details are a plain form, pre-filled from the offer email and checked against the floor salary from the Profile. That one comparison is factual, not advice.
  • Comparing and negotiating offers are explicitly out of scope for v1, and the screen says so instead of hinting at a missing feature.
Design notes
  • Automatic moves are visible and reversible. The banner shows from→to, when it happened, Undo and "Looks right". The full email and the match signals sit right below.
  • A Bulgarian email gets a one-line English summary. The original stays primary, so nothing is lost in translation.
  • Undo restores the Status and the cancelled Follow-up and teaches the matcher. Low-confidence matches never reach this state; they go to "Needs you" as an unmatched email.
