Design notes
  • Focal point is the "Needs you" queue: at most a handful of items, each with one primary action and a grey "why it's here" line, like the Feed's why-line. Stats sit above it but stay small. They're for motivation, not for work.
  • Queue order: oldest first, so nothing rots. The four kinds are a due Follow-up, an email to confirm, an upcoming interview, and a logged rejection waiting for a lesson. Items that are only coming up (due tomorrow) get one quiet line, not a card.
  • Follow-up due dates are starting defaults from the old prototype's cadence: 7 days after Applied, 3 after Screening, 2 after Interviewing, 1 after Offer (a note to self). They can be changed in Settings. No source cites the numbers.
  • The email-to-confirm case matches a Package, not an Application. Kris approved it and probably submitted on his phone. The app asks; it never creates or moves anything on a low-confidence match.
  • Grouped list: Interviewing, Screening, Offer and Applied are open. Closed (Rejected and Withdrawn) is collapsed by default. Each row shows Status as an inline select, so a manual change is one action. The last Event says who caused it ("email" or "you").
  • Applied shows 5 of 31 by default (soonest due first), then "Show 26 more". A long tail of quiet Applications shouldn't push Closed off the screen.
  • Stats: "What's working" draws sent vs replied per angle to scale, so a 1-of-4 design result can't pass for a trend. The label says "too few to tell".
Design notes
  • Reading local data is near-instant, so the skeleton only covers the Stats and the queue while new mail is checked (1–10 s). The cached list stays visible and usable underneath, and the layout doesn't jump.
  • One loading indicator only, the "Checking mail" text. No spinner on top of skeletons. Shimmer respects reduced motion (wf.css).
Design notes
  • Explains the model in two sentences: Applications appear by themselves when a Package is marked submitted, and emails keep them up to date. That sets the "nothing to log by hand" expectation from day one.
  • The primary action leads back to the Feed. If approved Packages exist, the secondary button names how many, because those are the fastest way to the first Application.
  • "Log a past application" is the one manual entry point. It exists so replies to applications from before apply-kit don't end up unmatched.
Design notes
  • Calm, not celebratory: a plain bordered box (not the dashed "empty" style), the next upcoming item, and when mail was last checked, so "nothing" reads as true and not as broken.
  • No confetti and no streak pressure. PRODUCT.md wants clarity and momentum at Track, not joy.
Design notes
  • "Never guess silently": an email that would change a Status but doesn't match confidently becomes a queue item. Nothing moves until Kris picks.
  • The likeliest candidates are listed with a plain reason each, then search, then "create a new Application" (for recruiter or off-tool applications), then "not about an application".
  • Two commit buttons separate matching from moving. Kris can file the email without accepting the Status change the classifier suggests.
  • The choice is learned per sender domain, so the same question isn't asked twice.
Design notes
  • At 200+ the queue collapses same-kind items ("6 follow-ups are due"). Each one is still reviewed separately, because the user sends every message.
  • Status filter chips carry counts. Angle and date-sent are selects, and the result line names every active filter with one "Clear".
  • Closed (153) stays collapsed and is never auto-deleted. Paging happens inside a group (Applied 1–25 of 29), so group headers stay put. Long titles truncate with the full text on hover.
  • Search covers job, company, contact and email text. It's useful when a recruiter calls and Kris needs to find the Application fast.
Design notes
  • Error format: what happened (sign-in expired, since when), why it's safe (mail is held and will be caught up), and the fix as three short steps that link to Settings → Mailbox.
  • "What still works" and what's paused are listed, so Kris doesn't stop trusting the whole screen. Stats are visibly marked as possibly incomplete.
  • The health indicator in the top bar turns red on every screen, not just here. The banner stays until the mailbox reconnects; it can't be dismissed.
  • The Follow-up card adds "check your inbox first": the app can't rule out a reply it can't see.
