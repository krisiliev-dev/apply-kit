                      Design notes
                      • Phase A starts from what exists, so the first screen asks for files, not typing. Every source except one CV is optional, and "No CV?" goes straight to the coach.
                      • The stepper names five phases so the 30–45 minute commitment is visible up front. Each phase saves as you go and can be stopped (see "Returning").
                      • The privacy box sits next to the drop zone, where the worry starts. It says what leaves the machine (extracted text to the model, non-China hosts) and what doesn't (the files).
                      • The LinkedIn help text explains how to get the export, because most people don't know it exists and it takes about 10 minutes to arrive.
                      • App nav stays live with Profile highlighted: onboarding is how the master profile gets built, and leaving never loses work.
                      Design notes
                      • Over 10 seconds, so: progress per file and overall, a time estimate, and the option to start reviewing pieces that are already done.
                      • Duplicates across CV versions are merged during import, so review shows each fact once.
                      • Cancelling keeps what was drafted. Skeleton cards match the real piece cards.
                      Design notes
                      • Partial failure doesn't block anything: the banner leads with what worked and the button still goes to review.
                      • The message names the cause (a scanned image) in plain words and gives three ways forward, each with its cost: text recognition (slow, misreads, so its pieces get flagged), a better file, or skip and let the interview cover the year.
                      • Text recognition runs locally, so a scanned CV is never uploaded as an image.
                      Design notes
                      • Three piece types share one card: Role, Achievement, Skill. Each shows its tags, its angle and where it came from, so you can judge it without opening the source.
                      • Bulk confirm works per role and skips flagged pieces, which saves time without letting a disputed claim through unseen.
                      • When sources disagree, both versions are quoted. "Ask me in the interview" passes the question to the coach instead of forcing a guess now.
                      • You don't have to finish: unchecked pieces go to the interview, where the coach covers them first. Discard is reversible from the Discarded filter.
                      Design notes
                      • With many old CVs the draft count explodes. Merging near-duplicates (with a "See merges" audit) and collapsing groups keep the review doable.
                      • Long piece text truncates in the collapsed view with the full text on hover; it wraps once the card is opened for editing.
                      Design notes
                      • Phase B has three jobs, shown as chips at the top: check what was imported, ask about timeline gaps, look for forgotten skills. The scope is visible so the interview doesn't feel endless.
                      • The gap question picks up the unreadable 2021 CV from the import, so the phases connect instead of starting over.
                      • The live panel shows every piece the coach writes. You can edit any of them, and nothing is invented: pieces only come from your answers.
                      • Near-the-line items (here, "about a third" turned into "33%") get a "Needs your OK" card that explains why, with both choices. Gaps are asked about, never filled in.
                      • "Finish interview" is always available. Questions you skip come back later as package questions, but only if a job needs them (concept C).
                      Design notes
                      • A model timeout is shown in a banner with an automatic retry (backoff) and a manual "Try now". Your typed answer stays on screen, marked "not sent yet".
                      • An alternative path: search setup needs no AI, so you can carry on with it.
                      Design notes
                      • One column of grouped fieldsets, top-aligned labels, optional fields marked "optional". Defaults come from PRODUCT.md, so most people only check them.
                      • Sofia and Remote EU are two equal checkboxes with "rank equally" written out. Relocation is a separate switch with its own countries, disabled until it's on, because it's a separate, lower-priority list.
                      • Dealbreakers say what they do ("hidden, with the reason shown"). The salary check runs on blur: an amount that looks yearly gets a warning with a suggested fix, not a blocking error.
                      • The mailbox step can be skipped because it's the only one that needs setup outside the app (an iCloud rule). It says what's read and what isn't.
                      Design notes
                      • The summary leads with numbers and says what's still open (2 pieces, mailbox skipped) without blocking. "Open your feed" is the main action.
                      • The "Keeps learning" box sets up phase C, so the first gap question inside a package isn't a surprise.
                      Design notes
                      • Returning mid-setup lands on where you stopped, with exact progress. "Skip setup" is the concept-C fallback for a day when you just want to apply.
