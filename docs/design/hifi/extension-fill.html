Fill this application

The Chrome side panel next to a company's portal. Left: the portal (a faint, dashed mock: the company's page, not ours). Right: apply-kit. It fills the page in front of you when you click, from your approved Package, master profile and answer bank, marks what it filled on the page, and asks about the rest. You check the page and press the portal's buttons yourself. All data is made up; the persona is Kris Marinov.

Let apply-kit fill the portals it knows

A one-time step. Chrome will ask you to allow apply-kit on these six job systems, so it can fill every page of an application and forms embedded in company sites.

  • Workday · myworkdayjobs.com, myworkdaysite.com
  • Greenhouse · greenhouse.io
  • Lever · lever.co
  • SmartRecruiters · smartrecruiters.com
  • Workable · workable.com
  • Ashby · ashbyhq.com
What this allows, and what it doesn'tapply-kit still does nothing on these sites until you click Fill. It never presses Next or Submit, never opens pages, and never sees your passwords. You can remove the permission in Chrome's extension settings at any time.

Without it, click the apply-kit icon in the toolbar on each new page before filling. Forms embedded in company sites can't be filled.

Design notes

  • Asked once, on the first Fill, not at install: the request arrives when its reason is on screen (ADR 0005). Chrome's own prompt follows the button.
  • The list names each site in words. "Not now" works but is honest about the cost (a click on the toolbar icon per page; embedded forms out of reach).

      • What needs Kris comes first, as a ready answer to accept; the filled list follows. Each row names its source in text (Profile, Answer bank, Package), never colour alone.

      • The panel can't see the page change by itself. When the tab's address or title changes, this list moves under "Earlier pages" and the panel says "New page? Click Fill this page", so a stale list never looks current.

            Design notes

            • A numbered checklist, because account creation is a sequence. The one step only Kris can do (the password) gets the most words and a way to find the field.
